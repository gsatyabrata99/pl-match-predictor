## 1. Margin-removal sensitivity

Same 760 matches (590 for Pinnacle) and same frozen model as the [bookmaker benchmark](2026-11-05-BOOKMAKER-BENCHMARK.md).

| Market, method | Market log loss | Brier | Mean favourite prob | Mean draw prob | Model − market (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Average closing, proportional | 0.9893 | 0.5915 | 0.531 | 0.241 | +0.0099 [−0.0025, +0.0216] |
| Average closing, Shin | 0.9895 | 0.5919 | 0.539 | 0.237 | +0.0096 [−0.0033, +0.0218] |
| Average closing, power | 0.9899 | 0.5922 | 0.542 | 0.235 | +0.0092 [−0.0040, +0.0217] |
| Pinnacle closing, proportional | 0.9739 | 0.5808 | 0.537 | 0.239 | +0.0083 [−0.0067, +0.0236] |
| Pinnacle closing, Shin | 0.9738 | 0.5809 | 0.542 | 0.237 | +0.0084 [−0.0071, +0.0240] |
| Pinnacle closing, power | 0.9739 | 0.5810 | 0.544 | 0.236 | +0.0083 [−0.0074, +0.0240] |

(Model log loss: 0.9991 on 760 matches, 0.9822 on the Pinnacle 590.)

**The method is immaterial here.** Market log loss moves by at most 0.0006, and the model-versus-market gap stays between +0.0083 and +0.0099. Shin and power do shift probability from longshots and draws toward favourites as expected, but in these high-draw seasons that did not make the market more accurate. The proportional method, used in the benchmark, stays the reference.

## 2. Under-confidence and temperature scaling

Temperature scaling sharpens (T < 1) or flattens (T > 1) the model's 1X2 probabilities without changing team ratings or the order of outcomes, so 1X2 accuracy cannot change (confirmed: identical in every season).

**Diagnostic: the best T for each season, fitted on that season's own results**

| 2022-23 | 2023-24 | 2024-25 | 2025-26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.13 (too sharp) | 0.81 (too flat) | 0.85 (too flat) | 1.20 (too sharp) |

**There is no consistent miscalibration.** The direction flips between seasons, and the average is close to 1. The under-confidence seen against the market in 2024-26 is not a stable property of the model's calibration against outcomes.

**Chronological test: fit T on earlier seasons, apply to the next**

| Season | Season-ahead T | Log loss change (95% CI) | Expanding-window T | Log loss change (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023-24 | 1.13 (from 22-23) | +0.0066 [+0.0013, +0.0115] | 1.13 | +0.0066 [+0.0013, +0.0115] |
| 2024-25 | 0.81 (from 23-24) | −0.0028 [−0.0118, +0.0069] | 0.96 | −0.0013 [−0.0030, +0.0006] |
| 2025-26 | 0.85 (from 24-25) | +0.0077 [−0.0001, +0.0150] | 0.92 | +0.0030 [−0.0007, +0.0063] |
| **Pooled (1,140)** | | **+0.0039 [−0.0004, +0.0082]** | | **+0.0028 [+0.0007, +0.0049]** |

Positive = worse. **Temperature scaling made predictions worse.** Each season's T is a poor guide to the next, so applying it adds error: 2 of 3 seasons worse under both schemes, and the expanding-window version is worse pooled with an interval that excludes zero. The improvement does not persist across seasons.

## Interpretation

The two findings fit together. Against the market, the model looks under-confident (slope 0.85). Against outcomes, it is roughly calibrated on average but drifts season to season. So the market's advantage is not that it is sharper across the board, which one global T could copy. It is that the market knows *which* matches deserve extreme probabilities: better discrimination, most plausibly from information the model lacks (team news, lineups, injuries, recent form). That is a resolution problem, not a calibration problem.

## Conclusions

1. Keep proportional margin removal. The method choice moves the benchmark by under 0.001.
2. Do not add temperature scaling. It fails chronological validation.
3. Improvement has to come from information the model doesn't have yet. The best-motivated next experiment is recency weighting (faster response to changes in team strength), validated chronologically and then prospectively.
