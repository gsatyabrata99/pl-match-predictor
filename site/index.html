How it works

1

Measure chances, not just goals

Every shot gets an expected goals (xG) value: the chance a typical player scores from that position and situation. A penalty is about 0.79 xG; a shot from 30 yards is about 0.03. Summing a team's xG tells you how good its chances were, which predicts future goals better than the goals themselves, because finishing luck evens out.

The model counts each match as 70% xG and 30% actual goals. Matches where xG isn't available yet count as goals only, at half weight.

2

Rate attack and defence, adjusted for opponents

Each team gets an attack rating (how much it creates compared with average) and a defence rating (how much it concedes compared with average, so lower is better). Scoring two against the bottom side counts for less than two against the best defence, so the ratings are solved together until they settle.

3

Start from last season, then let this season take over

Nine games is a small sample, so each team starts from last season's rating, pulled 25% toward average. That starting point counts like extra games. As this season's matches pile up, they outweigh it. Promoted sides start from the average of the three teams that went down last season.

4

Turn ratings into expected goals for this match

home xG = league home average × home attack × away defence. The away side works the same way with the away average. Home advantage comes from the gap between the two league averages ().

5

Turn expected goals into scorelines

Goals in football follow a Poisson distribution closely: if a team expects 1.5 goals, there's a 22% chance it scores none, 33% one, 25% two, and so on. Combining both teams gives the chance of every scoreline, and adding up the cells gives win, draw and loss.

The model supports the Dixon-Coles correction, a small adjustment to 0–0, 1–0, 0–1 and 1–1 used in academic and betting models.

What it can't see

Injuries, suspensions, rotation before European nights, new managers and late team news are all invisible to the model. Treat a 45% favourite as genuinely uncertain: across a season that side loses or draws more often than it wins.
